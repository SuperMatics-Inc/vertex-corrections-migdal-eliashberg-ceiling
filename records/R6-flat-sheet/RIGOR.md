# R6: Rigorous Analysis of the Universal First-Crossed-Vertex Onset Bound

This document analyzes the universal first-crossed-vertex onset problem beyond the spherical joint-overlap model of the records `R2`, `R3` and the version 1 preprint (Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.24407163`, which left both a universal crossed-skeleton bound and a counterexample open). We establish a sharp geometric dichotomy for the linearized instability temperature $T_{\mathrm{lin}}$ of the specified normal branch:

1. **Theorem 1 (Negative Result / Flat-Sheet Counterexample, proof draft for $m \to 0$ scaling limit + NUMERICAL eigenvalue crossing + CERTIFIED (padded binary64) finite-$m$ Rayleigh lower bound at fixed numerical normal branch):** Bounded density of states ($A = 1$) and a momentum-independent bare phonon interaction $V_j(\mathbf{q}) = K_j / N_0$ at fixed chemical potential are **insufficient** to preserve the adiabatic Migdal-Eliashberg ceiling $k_B T_{\mathrm{lin}} \le (1 + o(1)) C_\infty \sqrt{A M}$ as $m = \Omega/W \to 0$ ($C_\infty = 0.18272624777232952$). On 1D periodic bands and 2D parallel-flat-sheet bands (with $A = 1$), the first-crossed skeleton converges to an $O(1)$ non-vanishing $m \to 0$ scaling limit in which the exact-symmetric linearized pairing operator crosses above $T_{\mathrm{ceil}} = C_\infty \sqrt{A M}$ at moderate-to-strong coupling: at $\eta = T/T_{\mathrm{ceil}} = 1.000$, $\mathrm{Full} \approx 1.0030\text{--}1.0042$ ($\lambda = 4$, $\eta_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 1.0089$), $\mathrm{Full} \approx 1.0135\text{--}1.0141$ ($\lambda = 6$, $\eta_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 1.0456$), and $\mathrm{Full} \approx 1.0146\text{--}1.0164$ ($\lambda = 8$, $\eta_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 1.0651$). The exact-symmetric finite-$m$ periodic lattice sequence ($m = 0.020 \to 0.005$) approaches the continuum limit monotonically from below.
2. **Theorem 2 (Positive Universal Theorem on Curved Fermi Surfaces, proof draft + CERTIFIED rational/interval constants in `replay.py`):** Under bounded DOS ($N(\xi) \le A N_0$) plus a quantitative non-nesting / coarea-curvature strip overlap hypothesis (H3) -- which Lemma 1 proves holds on all $d \ge 2$ compact $C^2$ dispersions having a collar around $\Sigma_0$ with nonvanishing Fermi velocity, strictly positive principal curvatures, and non-degenerate pocket tangencies -- BOTH the normal crossed self-energy $\|\Sigma_X\|_\infty/\nu$ and the signed crossed pairing form $|F_X[y]|/Q_0[y]$ vanish uniformly as $O((A v_{\max} R)^2 x^\alpha (1 + \log_+(1/x))^{3+\beta})$ as $x = \pi \tau / W \to 0$. Combined with (a) the Young-ball high-temperature contraction for momentum-independent transfer and particle-hole-even DOS ($A R \le 1/9$, $N(-\xi) = N(\xi)$) and (b) the finite-DOS scalar ME theorem (the earlier curved-surface record), this bounds the adiabatic onset $T_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\mathrm{vertex}}$ by $(1 + o(1)) C_\infty \sqrt{A M}$ for even DOS with momentum-independent transfer in the small-$x$ curved-FS window, and bounds the shift relative to $T_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\mathrm{ME}}$ (or the asymmetric earlier-record ceiling $\sqrt{AM}/(\pi\sqrt{2})$) under the stated normal-branch hypothesis.

## 1. Setup and Exact Signed Skeleton

Use energy units $\tau = k_B T$, $\nu = \pi \tau$, $\omega_n = (2n+1)\nu$, $x = \nu / W$. Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a compact $d$-dimensional momentum domain with normalized measure $d\mu(\mathbf{k})$ ($\int_{\mathcal{B}} d\mu = 1$) and inversion-even dispersion $\xi(-\mathbf{k}) = \xi(\mathbf{k}) \in [-W, W]$ having normalized density of states $N(\xi)$ with $0 < N_0 = N(0) < \infty$ and $A := \operatorname{ess\,sup}_{\xi} N(\xi)/N_0 < \infty$. Let $d\lambda(\Omega)$ be a nonnegative phonon spectral measure on $(0, \Omega_{\max}]$ of finite positive mass $\lambda$, $M = \int \Omega^2 d\lambda(\Omega)$, $K_j = \int \frac{\Omega^2}{\Omega^2 + (2\nu j)^2} d\lambda(\Omega)$, $R = \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} K_j$.

Take bare phonon, fixed bare chemical potential, no Coulomb term, and precisely the rainbow plus first-crossed normal and anomalous skeleton (Schrodi-Oppeneer-Aperis, arXiv:1911.12872v2, Eq. (4)):
$$z_p = (i\omega_p - \xi_p - \Sigma_p)^{-1} = r_p - i Q_p, \qquad u_p = \omega_p - \operatorname{Im}\Sigma_p, \qquad Q_p = u_p |z_p|^2,$$
with $p = (\mathbf{k}, n)$, $s = (\mathbf{q}, j)$, $t = (\mathbf{q}', l)$, $a = p - s$, $b = p - s - t$, $c = p - t$, and symmetric positive interaction $0 \le V_j(\mathbf{q}) = V_j(-\mathbf{q}) = V_{-j}(\mathbf{q}) \le v_{\max} K_j / N_0$ ($v_{\max} = 1$ for momentum-independent transfer).

On any sign-preserving normal branch ($\omega_p Q_p > 0$), write $y_p = \varphi_p / u_p$ for a real frequency-even, inversion-even singlet pairing function $\varphi_p$ (so $y_p$ is real, frequency-odd, and inversion-even) and $h_p = \varphi_p |z_p|^2 = Q_p y_p$. Subtracting the imaginary part of the normal equation
$$\Sigma_p = \tau \sum_s V_s z_a + \tau^2 \sum_{s,t} V_s V_t z_a z_b z_c$$
from the linearized anomalous equation
$$\varphi_p = \tau \sum_s V_s h_a + \tau^2 \sum_{s,t} V_s V_t \big[ \operatorname{Re}(z_b z_c) h_a + \operatorname{Re}(z_a z_c^*) h_b + \operatorname{Re}(z_a z_b) h_c \big]$$
and testing against $h_p = Q_p y_p$ yields the exact signed quadratic form (`R2` record, §1; Eq. (292) of the version 1 manuscript):
$$F[y] = Q_0[y] + F_{\mathrm{ME}}[y] + F_X[y],$$
where
$$Q_0[y] := \sum_p \omega_p Q_p y_p^2 = \sum_p |\omega_p| |Q_p| y_p^2, \qquad F_{\mathrm{ME}}[y] := \frac{\tau}{2} \sum_{p,s} V_s Q_p Q_a (y_p - y_a)^2,$$
and the full crossed form $F_X[y]$ is the exact sum of a four-corner alternating square and three real-line-weighted pair squares:
$$F_X[y] = \tau^2 \sum_{p,s,t} V_s V_t \left[ -\frac{1}{4} Q_p Q_a Q_b Q_c (y_p - y_a + y_b - y_c)^2 + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{v \in \{a,b,c\}} Q_p Q_v r_{v'} r_{v''} (y_p - y_v)^2 \right],$$
where $\{v', v''\} = \{a, b, c\} \setminus \{v\}$. (Indeed, $-\operatorname{Im}(z_a z_b z_c) = Q_a r_b r_c + r_a Q_b r_c + r_a r_b Q_c - Q_a Q_b Q_c$; averaging the $-Q_p Q_a Q_b Q_c y_p (y_p - y_a + y_b - y_c)$ piece over the four anchors $(p,s,t)$, $(a,-s,t)$, $(b,-s,-t)$, $(c,s,-t)$ gives $-\frac{1}{4}(y_p - y_a + y_b - y_c)^2$, and reanchoring each two-real-line term between $p$ and $v$ gives $+\frac{1}{2} Q_p Q_v r_{v'} r_{v''} (y_p - y_v)^2$.)

In the symmetric pairing variable $d_p = |z_p|\varphi_p = |z_p| u_p y_p$, $F[y] = \langle d, (I - \mathcal{B}) d \rangle$, where $\mathcal{B}$ is the self-adjoint linearized pairing kernel with respect to the unweighted $l^2$ inner product $\langle d, d' \rangle = \sum_p d_p d'_p$, and $\sup\sigma(\mathcal{B}) > 1$ iff $\inf_{y \ne 0} F[y] < 0$. Moreover, using $|Q_v|, |r_v| \le |z_v|$, $(y_p - y_a + y_b - y_c)^2 \le 4(y_p^2 + y_a^2 + y_b^2 + y_c^2)$, $(y_p - y_v)^2 \le 2(y_p^2 + y_v^2)$, and anchor permutation symmetry gives the exact reanchoring bound (`R3` record, §A3):
$$|F_X[y]| \le 10\, L_X\, Q_0[y], \qquad L_X := \sup_p \frac{\tau^2}{\nu} \sum_{s,t} V_s V_t |z_a| |z_b| |z_c|.$$

## 2. Theorem 1: Non-Vanishing Scaling Limit and Ceiling Violation on Flat-Sheet Bands

### 2.1. Exact 1D and 2D Flat-Sheet Models
Consider first the 1D periodic dispersion on $\mathcal{B}_1 = [-\pi, \pi)$ with normalized measure $dk/(2\pi)$:
$$\xi(k) = W\left(\frac{2|k|}{\pi} - 1\right) - \mu_0, \qquad k \in [-\pi, \pi), \quad |\mu_0| < W,$$
and momentum-independent Einstein interaction $V_j(q) = K_j / N_0$ with $N_0 = 1/(2W)$, $A = 1$, $K_j = \lambda \Omega^2 / (\Omega^2 + (2\nu j)^2)$, $M = \lambda \Omega^2$, and $T_{\mathrm{ceil}} = C_\infty \sqrt{\lambda}\,\Omega$. (Equivalently, take the 2D periodic dispersion $\xi(k_x, k_y) = W(2|k_x|/\pi - 1) - \mu_0$ on $[-\pi, \pi)^2$, which has the exact same DOS $A = 1$ and identical $k_y$-independent homogeneous normal and pairing equations.)

### 2.2. Non-Vanishing $m = \Omega/W \to 0$ Scaling Limit (proof draft)
Fix $\lambda > 0$ and $\eta = T / T_{\mathrm{ceil}} > 0$, so $\tau = \eta C_\infty \sqrt{\lambda}\,\Omega$, $\nu = \pi\tau$, and $m = \Omega/W \to 0$ (hence $x = \nu/W \to 0$). The Matsubara ratio $\Omega/(2\nu) = (2\pi \eta C_\infty \sqrt{\lambda})^{-1}$ is independent of $m$.

Introduce the rescaled energy coordinate $y \in \mathbb{R}$ centered at the two Fermi points $\pm k_F$ ($k_F = \frac{\pi}{2}(1 + \mu_0/W) \in (0, \pi)$):
$$k = \pm\left(k_F + \frac{\pi \nu}{2W} y\right), \qquad y = \frac{\xi(k)}{\nu} \in \left[-\frac{W + \mu_0}{\nu},\, \frac{W - \mu_0}{\nu}\right].$$
On the right sheet ($+$) $k - k_F = +\frac{\pi\nu}{2W} y$; on the left sheet ($-$) $k + k_F = -\frac{\pi\nu}{2W} y$. Because $V_j$ is momentum-independent, the double momentum integral over $k_a, k_c$ decomposes into the 8 sheet combinations $(\sigma_k, \sigma_a, \sigma_c) \in \{\pm\}^3$. For $\sigma_k = +$ and $y, y_a, y_c = O(1)$, evaluating $k_b = k_a + k_c - k \pmod{2\pi}$ gives:
- $(\sigma_a, \sigma_c) = (+,+)$: $k_b = k_F + \frac{\pi\nu}{2W}(y_a + y_c - y) \implies \sigma_b = +$, $\xi_b/\nu = y_a + y_c - y$ ($O(1)$);
- $(\sigma_a, \sigma_c) = (+,-)$: $k_b = -k_F + \frac{\pi\nu}{2W}(y_a - y_c - y) \implies \sigma_b = -$, $\xi_b/\nu = y_c - y_a + y$ ($O(1)$);
- $(\sigma_a, \sigma_c) = (-,+)$: $k_b = -k_F + \frac{\pi\nu}{2W}(-y_a + y_c - y) \implies \sigma_b = -$, $\xi_b/\nu = y_a - y_c + y$ ($O(1)$);
- $(\sigma_a, \sigma_c) = (-,-)$: $k_b = -3 k_F + O(m)$. Provided $4 k_F \notin 2\pi \mathbb{Z}$ (e.g. $\mu_0 = -0.4 W$, where $k_F = 0.3\pi$ and $-3k_F = -0.9\pi \ne \pm 0.3\pi \pmod{2\pi}$), $k_b$ is separated by $\Theta(1)$ from $\pm k_F$, so $|\xi_b| = \Theta(W)$ and $\nu |z_b| = O(m) \to 0$.

On each sheet, $dk/(2\pi) = \frac{1}{2} N_0 d\xi = \frac{1}{2} N_0 \nu dy$, so the total sheet weights are $\alpha_f = \alpha_b = 1/2$ ($\alpha_f = 1/2$ per sheet). Writing $\tilde{z}(n, y) := \nu z_n(\nu y) = (i w_n - y - \tilde{\Sigma}(n, y))^{-1}$ with $w_n = 2n+1$ and $\tilde{\Sigma} = \Sigma/\nu$, the rainbow and crossed prefactors $\frac{\tau K_j}{\nu N_0} \cdot (\frac{1}{2} N_0 \nu) = \frac{1}{2\pi} K_j$ and $\frac{\tau^2 K_j K_l}{\nu N_0^2} \cdot (\frac{1}{2} N_0 \nu)^2 = \frac{1}{4\pi^2} K_j K_l$ are **completely independent of $m = \Omega/W$**.

Care with the $y$-integral in $\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{RB}}$ at $\mu_0 \ne 0$: on the finite band $[-W-\mu_0, W-\mu_0]$, $\operatorname{Re} \Sigma_{\mathrm{RB}}(n) = -\frac{\tau}{N_0}\sum_j K_j \cdot N_0 \log\frac{W-\mu_0}{W+\mu_0} + O(m \nu) = -\frac{\nu}{\pi} R \log\frac{W-\mu_0}{W+\mu_0} + O(m\nu)$. In units of $W$, $\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_{\mathrm{RB}}/W \to 0$, while in units of $\nu$, $\operatorname{Re}\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{RB}} = -\frac{R}{\pi}\log\frac{W-\mu_0}{W+\mu_0} = \Theta(1)$ is **$n$-independent** (up to $O(m)$). Because the infinite-line equations depend on $y$ only through $y + \operatorname{Re}\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{RB}}$, shifting $y \mapsto y - \operatorname{Re}\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{RB}}$ eliminates this $n$-independent constant identically by $y$-translation invariance on $\mathbb{R}$. Consequently, on $\mathbb{R}$, the $m \to 0$ scaling-limit self-energy $\tilde{\Sigma}(n, y) = \tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{RB}}(n) + \tilde{\Sigma}_X(n, y)$ solves:
$$\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{RB}}(n) = \frac{1}{\pi} \sum_{j \in \mathbb{Z}} K_j \lim_{Y\to\infty} \int_{-Y}^{Y} \tilde{z}(n - j, y_a)\, dy_a \quad (\text{purely imaginary; } \tilde{z}_0 \text{ gives } -i\operatorname{sgn}(w_{n-j})),$$
$$\tilde{\Sigma}_X(n, y) = +\frac{1}{\pi^2} \sum_{j,l \in \mathbb{Z}} K_j K_l \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{dy_a\, dy_c}{4} \Big[ \tilde{z}(a, y_a) \tilde{z}(c, y_c) \tilde{z}(b, y_a + y_c - y) + 2 \tilde{z}(a, y_a) \tilde{z}(c, y_c) \tilde{z}(b, y_a - y_c + y) \Big],$$
where $a = n-j$, $c = n-l$, $b = n-j-l$ (and $\frac{1}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}$; the positive sign $+1/\pi^2$ matches $\Sigma_X = +\tau^2 \sum V_s V_t z_a z_b z_c$!).

### 2.3. Exact Non-Vanishing of Bare $\Sigma_X^{(0)}$ (proof draft, `NUMERICAL` 2D-FFT cross-check in `replay.py`)
For the bare propagator $\tilde{z}_0(m, y) = (i w_m - y)^{-1}$, evaluate the channel-1 integral at $y \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$I_1(w_a, w_b, w_c; y) := \iint_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{dy_a}{2\pi} \frac{dy_c}{2\pi} \frac{1}{i w_a - y_a} \frac{1}{i w_c - y_c} \frac{1}{i w_b - (y_a + y_c - y)}.$$
Closing the $y_a$-contour in $\operatorname{Im} y_a > 0$ (when $w_a > 0$) or $\operatorname{Im} y_a < 0$ (when $w_a < 0$), the $y_a$-integral vanishes unless $\operatorname{sgn}(w_a) = -\operatorname{sgn}(w_b)$, in which case it equals $\frac{i\operatorname{sgn}(w_a)}{y_c - y + i(w_a - w_b)}$. Closing the $y_c$-contour next vanishes unless $\operatorname{sgn}(w_c) = \operatorname{sgn}(w_a)$, yielding:
$$I_1(w_a, w_b, w_c; y) = \frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{\operatorname{sgn}(w_a) = \operatorname{sgn}(w_c) = -\operatorname{sgn}(w_b)\}}}{i(w_a + w_c - w_b) - y}.$$
For channels 2 and 3 ($y_b = y_a - y_c + y$), substituting $u_c = -y_c$ gives $-I_1(w_a, w_b, -w_c; -y)$:
$$I_{23}(w_a, w_b, w_c; y) = -\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{\operatorname{sgn}(w_a) = -\operatorname{sgn}(w_c) = -\operatorname{sgn}(w_b)\}}}{i(w_a - w_c - w_b) + y}.$$
All three surviving channels remain $\Theta(1)$ as $m \to 0$. In `replay.py` (mode `NUMERICAL`), this exact formula matches an independent 2D FFT convolution on $[-2000, 2000]$ to $2.391 \times 10^{-4}$ (and rejects the `residue_sign` mutant at exit 1).

## 3. Theorem 2: Universal Adiabatic Bound on Curved Non-Nested Fermi Surfaces ($d \ge 2$)

### 3.1. Hypotheses
Assume:
- **(H1) Bounded even DOS:** Compact $d$-dimensional momentum space $\mathcal{B}$ ($d \ge 2$), inversion-even dispersion $\xi(-\mathbf{k}) = \xi(\mathbf{k}) \in [-W, W]$ with normalized DOS $N(\xi) \le A N_0$, $0 < N_0 = N(0) < \infty$, $A < \infty$.
- **(H2) Interaction:** Bare phonon spectrum $d\lambda(\Omega)$ on $(0, \Omega_{\max}]$, $0 \le V_j(\mathbf{q}) = V_j(-\mathbf{q}) = V_{-j}(\mathbf{q}) \le v_{\max} K_j / N_0$.
- **(H3) Uniform Three-Line Strip Overlap Bound:** There exist $C_{\mathrm{geom}} < \infty$, $\alpha \in (0, 1]$, and $\beta \ge 0$ such that for all $u_a, u_b, u_c \in (0, W]$,
  $$\sup_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}} I_{\mathbf{k}}(u_a, u_b, u_c) := \sup_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}} \int_{\mathcal{B}^2} \mathbf{1}_{|\xi(\mathbf{k}_a)| \le u_a}\, \mathbf{1}_{|\xi(\mathbf{k}_b)| \le u_b}\, \mathbf{1}_{|\xi(\mathbf{k}_c)| \le u_c}\, d\mu(\mathbf{k}_a)\, d\mu(\mathbf{k}_c)$$
  (with $\mathbf{k}_b = \mathbf{k}_a + \mathbf{k}_c - \mathbf{k}$) satisfies
  $$\sup_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}} I_{\mathbf{k}}(u_a, u_b, u_c) \le C_{\mathrm{geom}} (A N_0)^2 \frac{u_a u_b u_c}{W^\alpha \min(u_a, u_b, u_c)^{1-\alpha}} \left(1 + \log_+\frac{W}{\min(u_a, u_b, u_c)}\right)^\beta.$$

### 3.2. Lemma 1 (Coarea-Curvature Sufficiency for (H3), proof draft)
**Statement:** Let $\xi \in C^2(\mathcal{B})$ ($d \ge 2$) have an energy collar $\{|\xi| \le \varepsilon_0\}$ ($\varepsilon_0 > 0$) on which $|\nabla\xi(\mathbf{k})| \ge v_{\min} > 0$, $\|D^2\xi\|_\infty \le M_2$, and each energy level set $\Sigma_\xi$ is a finite disjoint union of smooth strictly convex $C^2$ hypersurfaces with all principal curvatures $\ge \kappa_{\min} > 0$ (and if $\Sigma_0$ has more than one connected component, any distinct-component critical pair $(\mathbf{k}_b^*, \mathbf{k}_c^*)$ with parallel/antiparallel normals satisfies non-degenerate relative curvature $\det(\Pi_b(\mathbf{k}_b^*) \mp \Pi_c(\mathbf{k}_c^*)) \ne 0$). Then **(H3)** holds with:
- $\alpha = 1, \beta = 1$ in $d = 2$;
- $\alpha = 1, \beta = 0$ in $d \ge 3$.
*(Note: The unproved arbitrary degeneracy-order-$p$ claim in v1 is dropped.)*

**Proof:**
First, $I_{\mathbf{k}}(u_a, u_b, u_c)$ is invariant under every permutation of $(u_a, u_b, u_c)$: swapping $\mathbf{k}_a \leftrightarrow \mathbf{k}_c$ leaves $\mathbf{k}_b = \mathbf{k}_a + \mathbf{k}_c - \mathbf{k}$ invariant, while the volume-preserving involution $(\mathbf{k}_a, \mathbf{k}_c) \mapsto (-\mathbf{k}_b, \mathbf{k}_c)$ satisfies $-\mathbf{k}_b + \mathbf{k}_c - \mathbf{k} = -\mathbf{k}_a$ and $\xi(-\mathbf{k}_a) = \xi(\mathbf{k}_a)$, $\xi(-\mathbf{k}_b) = \xi(\mathbf{k}_b)$ by inversion symmetry, swapping the roles of $u_a$ and $u_b$. Hence we may assume $u_c = u_1 \le u_b = u_2 \le u_a = u_3$ without loss of generality.

If $u_3 \ge \varepsilon_0$, dropping $\mathbf{1}_{|\xi_a|\le u_3}$ and integrating $\mathbf{k}_c, \mathbf{k}_a$ (using $\mathbf{k}_b = \mathbf{k}_a + \mathbf{k}_c - \mathbf{k}$ with Jacobian 1 and $N(\xi) \le A N_0$) gives $I_{\mathbf{k}} \le (2 A N_0 u_1)(2 A N_0 u_2) \le \frac{4W}{\varepsilon_0} (A N_0)^2 \frac{u_1 u_2 u_3}{W}$.
Suppose now $u_1 \le u_2 \le u_3 < \varepsilon_0$, so all three momenta lie inside the smooth collar. By the coarea formula on $\mathbf{k}_a$ and $\mathbf{k}_c$:
$$I_{\mathbf{k}}(u_a, u_b, u_c) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|^2} \int_{-u_3}^{u_3} d\xi_a \int_{-u_1}^{u_1} d\xi_c \int_{\Sigma_{\xi_a}} \frac{d\sigma(\mathbf{k}_a)}{|\nabla\xi(\mathbf{k}_a)|} \int_{\Sigma_{\xi_c}} \frac{d\sigma(\mathbf{k}_c)}{|\nabla\xi(\mathbf{k}_c)|}\, \mathbf{1}_{|\xi(\mathbf{k}_a + \mathbf{k}_c - \mathbf{k})| \le u_2}.$$
Fix $\xi_a \in [-u_3, u_3]$, $\xi_c \in [-u_1, u_1]$, and one pair of strictly convex components of $\Sigma_{\xi_a}$ and $\Sigma_{\xi_c}$ (summing over the finite component pairs multiplies $C_{\mathrm{geom}}$ by the squared component count). Let $\mathbf{v}_b = \nabla\xi(\mathbf{k}_b)$, $\mathbf{v}_c = \nabla\xi(\mathbf{k}_c)$, $\mathbf{n}_b = \mathbf{v}_b/|\mathbf{v}_b|$, $\mathbf{n}_c = \mathbf{v}_c/|\mathbf{v}_c|$, and split the $(\mathbf{k}_a, \mathbf{k}_c)$ domain into three regions for any $\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}$:

1. **Non-tangential region** ($|\sin\theta(\mathbf{v}_b, \mathbf{v}_c)| \ge \delta_0 > 0$): Along $\Sigma_{\xi_c}$, the tangential gradient of $g(\mathbf{k}_c) := \xi(\mathbf{k}_a + \mathbf{k}_c - \mathbf{k})$ satisfies $|\nabla_{\Sigma_{\xi_c}} g(\mathbf{k}_c)| = |\mathbf{v}_b| \sin\theta \ge v_{\min} \delta_0$. By the coarea formula on $\Sigma_{\xi_c}$, the $(d-1)$-measure of $\{|g| \le u_2\}$ is at most $C_1 u_2 / (v_{\min} \delta_0)$.

2. **Forward tangency ($\mathbf{n}_b \approx +\mathbf{n}_c$, valid for ALL $\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}$):** Let $\mathbf{w} := \mathbf{k}_a - \mathbf{k}$ and $r := |\mathbf{w}|$. On the same strictly convex component family, strict convexity implies the Gauss map $\mathbf{n}: \Sigma_\xi \to S^{d-1}$ is a global diffeomorphism with inverse Lipschitz constant $\le 1/\kappa_{\min}$, so for $r > r_0 > 0$ (and $u_2$ small) $|\mathbf{n}_b - \mathbf{n}_c| \ge c(r_0) > 0$, falling back into Region 1 (or, on distinct components with $\mathbf{n}_b^* = +\mathbf{n}_c^*$, the non-degenerate relative Hessian $|\mathbf{v}_b^*|(\Pi_b - \Pi_c)$ yields measure $O(u_2(1+\log_+(W/u_2)))$ in $d=2$ and $O(u_2)$ in $d \ge 3$). Thus assume $r \le r_0$ (with $r_0 > 0$ chosen small below).
   - *Inner cap $r \le r_* := C_1 u_2$* (with $C_1 := \frac{16 M_2}{v_{\min}^2 \kappa_{\min}} + \frac{4}{v_{\min}}$): Since $\Sigma_{\xi_a}$ is a smooth $C^2$ hypersurface, its intersection with the ball $|\mathbf{k}_a - \mathbf{k}| \le r_*$ has $(d-1)$-dimensional measure $O(r_*^{d-1}) = O(u_2^{d-1}) \le O(u_2)$ for all $d \ge 2$ (requiring zero assumption on $\mathbf{k}_c$).
   - *Outer collar $r \in (r_*, r_0]$:* Write $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{w}_T + \mathbf{w}_N$ relative to $T_{\mathbf{k}_c}\Sigma_{\xi_c} \oplus \mathbb{R}\mathbf{n}_c$. Since $|\xi(\mathbf{k}_c + \mathbf{w}) - \xi(\mathbf{k}_c)| \le 2 u_2$ and $|\xi(\mathbf{k}_c + \mathbf{w}) - \xi(\mathbf{k}_c) - \mathbf{v}_c \cdot \mathbf{w}| \le \frac{M_2}{2} r^2$, we have $|\mathbf{w}_N| \le \frac{2 u_2}{v_{\min}} + \frac{M_2}{2 v_{\min}} r^2$. For $r_0$ small enough that $\frac{2 u_2}{v_{\min}} + \frac{M_2}{2 v_{\min}} r^2 \le \frac{1}{2} r$, we have $|\mathbf{w}_T| \ge \frac{1}{2} r$. Now test the tangential gradient $\nabla_{\Sigma_{\xi_c}} g(\mathbf{k}_c) = P_{T}(\mathbf{v}_b)$ along $\mathbf{w}_T$: using $P_T(\mathbf{v}_c) = 0$ and $\mathbf{w}_T^T D^2\xi(\mathbf{k}_c)\mathbf{w}_T = |\mathbf{v}_c|\, \mathbf{w}_T^T \Pi_c(\mathbf{k}_c) \mathbf{w}_T \ge v_{\min} \kappa_{\min} |\mathbf{w}_T|^2$,
     $$\mathbf{w}_T \cdot \nabla_{\Sigma_{\xi_c}} g(\mathbf{k}_c) = \mathbf{w}_T^T D^2\xi(\mathbf{k}_c)\mathbf{w}_T + \mathbf{w}_T^T D^2\xi(\mathbf{k}_c)\mathbf{w}_N + O(\omega_\xi(r) r^2),$$
     where $\omega_\xi(r) \to 0$ is the modulus of continuity of $D^2\xi$. Because $|\mathbf{w}_T^T D^2\xi \mathbf{w}_N| \le M_2 r \big(\frac{2u_2}{v_{\min}} + \frac{M_2}{2v_{\min}} r^2\big)$ and $r \ge r_*$, the $u_2 r$ term is $\le \frac{1}{8} v_{\min}\kappa_{\min} r^2$; choosing $r_0$ small enough absorbs the $r^3$ and $\omega_\xi(r)r^2$ terms, giving $|\nabla_{\Sigma_{\xi_c}} g(\mathbf{k}_c)| \ge \frac{1}{8} v_{\min}\kappa_{\min} r$ for **all** $r \in (r_*, r_0]$. By the coarea formula on $\Sigma_{\xi_c}$, the $\mathbf{k}_c$-slice measure is $O(u_2 / r)$, and integrating over $r = |\mathbf{k}_a - \mathbf{k}| \in (r_*, r_0]$ on $\Sigma_{\xi_a}$ (whose $r$-annulus has measure $O(r^{d-2} dr)$) gives $O(u_2(1 + \log_+(W/u_2)))$ for $d = 2$ and $O(u_2)$ for $d \ge 3$.

3. **Antipodal tangency ($\mathbf{n}_b \approx -\mathbf{n}_c$, valid for ALL $\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}$):** If $|\sin\theta(\mathbf{v}_b, \mathbf{v}_a)| \ge \delta_0$, the tangential gradient along $\Sigma_{\xi_a}$ is $\ge v_{\min}\delta_0$, giving $\Sigma_{\xi_a}$-measure $O(u_2)$ directly. Thus it remains to bound neighborhoods of points $(\mathbf{k}_a^*, \mathbf{k}_c^*) \in \Sigma_{\xi_a} \times \Sigma_{\xi_c}$ where $\mathbf{n}_b^* = -\mathbf{n}_c^*$ and $\mathbf{n}_b^* = \pm\mathbf{n}_a^*$ (notice this holds for **all** $\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}$, not only $\mathbf{k} \in \Sigma_0$; e.g., on the 2D circle at $\mathbf{k} = (3k_F, 0)$ with $\mathbf{k}_a^* = \mathbf{k}_c^* = (k_F, 0)$ and $\mathbf{k}_b^* = (-k_F, 0)$).
   - If $\mathbf{n}_b^* = -\mathbf{n}_a^* = -\mathbf{n}_c^*$ (so $\mathbf{n}_a^* = +\mathbf{n}_c^*$): parameterize $\Sigma_{\xi_a}, \Sigma_{\xi_c}$ around $\mathbf{k}_a^*, \mathbf{k}_c^*$ by tangent coordinates $\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{d-1}$ with common unit normal $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{n}_a^* = \mathbf{n}_c^* = -\mathbf{n}_b^*$. Since $\mathbf{v}_b^* = -|\mathbf{v}_b^*|\mathbf{n}$, the quadratic expansion of $\xi(\mathbf{k}_a(\mathbf{t}) + \mathbf{k}_c(\mathbf{s}) - \mathbf{k})$ has coefficient $1$ (via $\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{v}_b^*|$) on every positive-definite second fundamental form:
     $$\xi(\mathbf{k}_b(\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{s})) = \xi(\mathbf{k}_b^*) + \frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{v}_b^*| \Big[ \mathbf{t}^T \Pi_a \mathbf{t} + \mathbf{s}^T \Pi_c \mathbf{s} + (\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{s})^T \Pi_b (\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{s}) \Big] + o((|\mathbf{t}|+|\mathbf{s}|)^2).$$
     Because the $2(d-1) \times 2(d-1)$ Hessian is uniformly positive definite ($\succeq v_{\min}\kappa_{\min} I_{2d-2}$), the slab $|\xi(\mathbf{k}_b)| \le u_2$ (a spherical/ellipsoidal shell around $|\xi(\mathbf{k}_b^*)|$ in $\mathbb{R}^{2d-2}$, shifted without widening by $\xi_a - \xi_c$) has $(2d-2)$-dimensional measure $O(u_2)$ for every $d \ge 2$.
   - If $\mathbf{n}_b^* = +\mathbf{n}_a^* = -\mathbf{n}_c^*$: on a single component this forces $\mathbf{n}_b^* = +\mathbf{n}_a^*$, so swapping the roles of $\mathbf{k}_a$ and $\mathbf{k}_c$ puts $\mathbf{w}' = \mathbf{k}_c - \mathbf{k}$ in Case 2 (forward tangency between $\mathbf{k}_b$ and $\mathbf{k}_a$, giving $O(u_2(1 + \log_+(W/u_2)))$ for $d=2$ and $O(u_2)$ for $d \ge 3$); on distinct components, non-degeneracy of $\Pi_b - \Pi_a$ again gives the same bound.

Multiplying by $\int_{-u_3}^{u_3} d\xi_a \int_{-u_1}^{u_1} d\xi_c = 4 u_1 u_3$ and normalizing by $(A N_0)^2$ establishes (H3) with $\alpha = 1, \beta = 1$ ($d=2$) and $\alpha = 1, \beta = 0$ ($d \ge 3$). $\square$

### 3.3. Lemma 2 (Exact Layer-Cake Reduction to $O(x^\alpha \log^{3+\beta}(1/x))$, proof draft; constants `CERTIFIED` in `replay.py`)
**Statement:** Assume (H1), (H2), (H3). Let $D_n(\xi) := (\omega_n^2 + \xi^2)^{-1/2}$. Then for every $\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{B}$ and all Matsubara indices $a, b, c$:
$$\mathcal{J}_3(\mathbf{k}; a,b,c) := \int_{\mathcal{B}^2} D_a(\xi_a)\, D_b(\xi_b)\, D_c(\xi_c)\, d\mu(\mathbf{k}_a)\, d\mu(\mathbf{k}_c) \le \frac{C_{\mathrm{LC}}}{\nu} (A N_0)^2 \, x^\alpha \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{x}\right)^{3 + \beta},$$
where $C_{\mathrm{LC}} := 3 C_{\mathrm{geom}} + 34$ (certified $24\sqrt{2} < 34$ in `replay.py`) and $x = \nu / W \le 1$.

**Proof:**
For any $m > 0$ and $\xi \in \mathbb{R}$, $(m^2 + \xi^2)^{-1/2} \le \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\max(m, |\xi|)} = \sqrt{2} \int_m^\infty \frac{du}{u^2}\, \mathbf{1}_{|\xi| \le u}$ (certified sharp at $|\xi|=m$ in `replay.py`). Multiplying the three factors and using $|\omega_n| \ge \nu$:
$$\mathcal{J}_3(\mathbf{k}; a,b,c) \le 2\sqrt{2} \int_\nu^\infty \frac{du_a}{u_a^2} \int_\nu^\infty \frac{du_b}{u_b^2} \int_\nu^\infty \frac{du_c}{u_c^2}\, I_{\mathbf{k}}(u_a, u_b, u_c).$$
- **Region I ($\max(u_a, u_b, u_c) \ge W$):** By symmetry of the upper bound, multiply the $u_c \ge W$ sector by $3$. Using $\int_{\mathcal{B}} \mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\le u} d\mu \le \min(2 A N_0 u, 1)$, $\frac{1}{2 A N_0} \le W$, $\int_\nu^\infty u^{-2}\min(2 A N_0 u, 1)\,du \le 2 A N_0 (1 + \log_+(W/\nu))$, and $\int_W^\infty u_c^{-2} du_c = 1/W$, Region I contributes at most
  $$2\sqrt{2} \cdot 3 \cdot \frac{4}{W} (A N_0)^2 \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = \frac{24\sqrt{2}}{\nu} (A N_0)^2 x \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{x}\right)^2 \le \frac{34}{\nu} (A N_0)^2 x^\alpha \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{x}\right)^{3+\beta}$$
  (since $x \le x^\alpha$ for $x \le 1$ and $24\sqrt{2} \approx 33.9411 < 34$).
- **Region II ($u_a, u_b, u_c \in [\nu, W)$):** Using (H3) and the uniform bound $\min(u_a, u_b, u_c)^{\alpha - 1} \le \nu^{\alpha - 1}$ for all $\alpha \in (0, 1]$ (avoiding any $1/(1-\alpha)$ singularity as $\alpha \to 1^-$) together with $\log_+(W/\min) \le \log_+(1/x)$:
  $$2\sqrt{2} \int_{[\nu, W)^3} \frac{du_a du_b du_c}{u_a^2 u_b^2 u_c^2}\, I_{\mathbf{k}} \le 2\sqrt{2}\, C_{\mathrm{geom}} \frac{(A N_0)^2}{W^\alpha \nu^{1-\alpha}} \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{x}\right)^\beta \left(\int_\nu^W \frac{du}{u}\right)^3 \le \frac{3 C_{\mathrm{geom}}}{\nu} (A N_0)^2 x^\alpha \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{x}\right)^{3+\beta}$$
  (since $2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.8284 < 3$).

Summing Regions I and II gives $C_{\mathrm{LC}} = 3 C_{\mathrm{geom}} + 34$ uniformly for all $\alpha \in (0, 1]$. $\square$

### 3.4. Theorem 2: Universal Adiabatic Bound Beyond the Spherical Model (proof draft)
Assume (H1), (H2), (H3) and put $B := \max(1, A v_{\max} R)$ (for $\tau \ge C_\infty\sqrt{AM}$, $R \le \lambda + \frac{2.5}{A}$, so $B = O(1)$ as $\rho = \Omega_{\max}/W \to 0$).

1. **All-$x$ Young-ball closure ($A R \le 1/9$ under $V_j(\mathbf{q}) = K_j/N_0$ and particle-hole-even DOS $N(-\xi) = N(\xi)$):** Under these two extra hypotheses (matching the `R3` record, §B, since otherwise $\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_{\mathrm{RB}}^{(0)} = \Theta(\nu A v_{\max} R \log(1/x))$ leaves the ball $\|\Sigma\|_\infty \le \nu/3$ as $x \to 0$), $\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_{\mathrm{RB}}^{(0)} \equiv 0$, so the triple-Hölder Young-ball contraction in $\|\Sigma\|_\infty \le \nu/3$ (giving $u_n/|\omega_n| \ge 2/3$, with $c_{\mathrm{Holder}} < 4$, image $< 0.3187 < 1/3$, Lipschitz $< 0.9340 < 1$ all certified in `replay.py`) and the Schur pairing bound $< 1$ hold unconditionally for **all** $x > 0$ without needing (H3). In particular, $A\lambda \le 1/26^2$ gives stability at all temperatures above $\tau_Y \le \frac{117}{667}\sqrt{AM} < C_\infty\sqrt{AM}$.

2. **Adiabatic small-$x$ curved-FS window:** Suppose a sign-preserving normal branch exists with $u_n(\mathbf{k}) \ge c_0 |\omega_n|$ ($c_0 \in (0, 1]$) and $|\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_n(\mathbf{k})| \le \Delta_R$ with $\Delta_R \le c_1 \nu$ (even-DOS / small-shift regime, e.g. $c_0 \ge 2/3, c_1 \le 1/3$ in the $AR \le 1/9$ Young ball, or small-$x$ perturbation of $\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_{\mathrm{RB}} \equiv 0$) or $\Delta_R \le C_{\mathrm{Re}}\nu(1 + \log_+(1/x))$ (asymmetric / general-$V_j$ regime). *(Explicit scope note: for $A R \le 1/9$ with $N(-\xi)=N(\xi)$ and $V_j = K_j/N_0$, existence/uniqueness with $c_0 \ge 2/3, c_1 \le 1/3$ is proved above; for $\lambda > 1/9$ or asymmetric/momentum-dependent bands, existence of the normal branch with these bounds is an explicit hypothesis of Theorem 2(2), not proved from (H1)–(H3) alone.)* Putting $\gamma := 1 + \Delta_R/(c_0\nu)$, $|\xi_p + \operatorname{Re}\Sigma_p| \le u$ implies $|\xi_p| \le \gamma u$ for $u \ge c_0\nu$, so $|z_p| \le \gamma \sqrt{2} \int_{c_0\nu}^\infty v^{-2}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi_p|\le v}\,dv$ and Lemma 2 (at cutoff $c_0\nu$) gives
   $$\|\Sigma_X\|_\infty \le \frac{\gamma^3 c_0^{\alpha-1} C_{\mathrm{LC}}}{\pi^2} (A v_{\max} R)^2 \, \nu \, x^\alpha \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{c_0 x}\right)^{3+\beta}, \qquad |F_X[y]| \le 10\, L_X\, Q_0[y], \quad L_X \le \frac{\gamma^3 c_0^{\alpha-1} C_{\mathrm{LC}}}{\pi^2} (A v_{\max} R)^2 \, x^\alpha \left(1 + \log_+\frac{1}{c_0 x}\right)^{3+\beta}.$$
   (When $\Delta_R \le c_1\nu$, $\gamma = 1 + c_1/c_0 = O(1)$; when $\Delta_R \le C_{\mathrm{Re}}\nu(1+\log_+(1/x))$, $\gamma^3$ adds at most $3$ powers of $\log_+(1/x)$, preserving $x^\alpha$-vanishing as $x \to 0$.) Consequently, in the small-$x$ curved-FS window $x \le x_*(B, \text{geom})$:
   - **(i) Momentum-independent interaction ($v_{\max}=1$, $V_j(\mathbf{q}) = K_j/N_0$):** Decomposing $y = \bar{y} + y_\perp$ into its $|Q|$-weighted Fermi-surface mean and orthogonal variance ($\int_{\mathcal{B}} Q_n(\mathbf{k}) y_{\perp,n}(\mathbf{k})\, d\mu(\mathbf{k}) = 0$) cancels the cross term in $Q_0$ and $F_{\mathrm{ME}}$ identically. On the $y_\perp$ sector, writing $\bar{Q}_n = \int_{\mathcal{B}} Q_n(\mathbf{k}) d\mu(\mathbf{k}) = \pi A N_0 b_n$ ($\bar{Q}_{-n-1} = -\bar{Q}_n$, $b_n \in (0, 1]$), folding $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ onto $m \ge 0$ gives $F_{\mathrm{ME}}[y_\perp] = 2 \sum_{n\ge 0} (\tau \sum_{m\ge 0} \frac{P_{nm}}{N_0} \bar{Q}_m) \int |Q_n(\mathbf{k})| y_{\perp,n}(\mathbf{k})^2 d\mu(\mathbf{k}) \ge 0$ because $P_{nm} = K_{|n-m|} - K_{n+m+1} \ge 0$ and $\bar{Q}_m > 0$. On the $\bar{y}$ sector, $Q_0[\bar{y}] + F_{\mathrm{ME}}[\bar{y}; u] = 2\pi A N_0 \nu \langle \tilde{x}, (I - S(b)) \tilde{x}\rangle$ with $\tilde{x}_n = \sqrt{w_n b_n}\, \bar{y}_n$ and Eq. (1) transfer of the earlier curved-surface record $A K_j$. Hence
     $$F[y] \ge \big(1 - \sup\sigma S(b) - 10 L_X\big) Q_0[\bar{y}] + (1 - 10 L_X) Q_0[y_\perp],$$
     where $b_n := \frac{1}{\pi A N_0} \int_{\mathcal{B}} \frac{u_n(\mathbf{k})\, d\mu(\mathbf{k})}{u_n(\mathbf{k})^2 + (\xi_{\mathbf{k}} + \operatorname{Re}\Sigma_n(\mathbf{k}))^2} \in (0, 1]$ and $S(b)$ uses transfer $A K_j$ (the earlier curved-surface record).
     - If $N(-\xi) = N(\xi)$ (even DOS) and $\Sigma_n(\mathbf{k}) = \Sigma_{\mathrm{RB},n} + \Sigma_{X,n}(\mathbf{k})$ lies within $O(\nu L_X)$ of the purely imaginary rainbow solution ($\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_{\mathrm{RB}} \equiv 0$, weights $b_n^*$), then $\|b - b^*\|_\infty = O(L_X)$. Because $\sup\sigma S(b)$ is locally Lipschitz in $b$ around $b^*$ on the core Matsubara indices $|n| < N_c$ while the Matsubara tail $|n| \ge N_c$ contributes $O(K_{N_c}) \to 0$ in operator norm, $|\sup\sigma S(b) - \sup\sigma S(b^*)| = O(\|b - b^*\|_\infty) = O(L_X)$ uniformly near $b^*$; Theorem (a) of the earlier curved-surface record gives $\sup\sigma(S(b^*)) < 1$ with quantitative margin $\Omega(\frac{\tau - T_{\mathrm{ceil}}}{T_{\mathrm{ceil}}})$ above $T_{\mathrm{ceil}} = C_\infty\sqrt{AM}$ (when $L_X \ll \frac{\tau - T_{\mathrm{ceil}}}{T_{\mathrm{ceil}}}$), hence $\tau_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\mathrm{vertex}} \le C_\infty\sqrt{AM}\big(1 + C_3 B^2 \rho^\alpha (1 + \log_+(1/\rho))^{3+\beta}\big)$ as $\rho = \Omega_{\max}/W \to 0$.
     - If $N(-\xi) \ne N(\xi)$ (general asymmetric DOS, where $b_n \le 1$ does **not** imply $\sup\sigma S(b) \le \sup\sigma S(1)$ because $S(b)$ carries a negative normal-mass diagonal), then relative to Theorem (c) of the earlier curved-surface record ($\sup\sigma S(b) < 1$ for all $\tau \ge \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{AM}$ on every positive time-reversal-reflected branch), $T_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\mathrm{vertex}} \le \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{AM}\big(1 + C_3 B^2 \rho^\alpha (1 + \log_+(1/\rho))^{3+\beta}\big)$ (and shifts $T_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\mathrm{ME}}$ evaluated on the same branch by at most $1 + O(L_X)$).
   - **(ii) General momentum-dependent interaction ($0 \le V_j(\mathbf{q}) \le v_{\max} K_j / N_0$):** $F[y] \ge Q_0[y] + F_{\mathrm{ME}}[y; u] - 10 L_X Q_0[y]$, so the first-crossed vertex shifts the momentum-resolved ME pairing onset $T_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\mathrm{ME}}$ on that branch by at most a relative factor $1 + O((A v_{\max} R)^2 \rho^\alpha (1+\log_+(1/\rho))^{3+\beta})$ in the small-$x$ curved-FS window.

**Open Boundaries:** (1) For $\lambda \ge 1/9$ at intermediate $x > x_*$, a global non-perturbative closure without spherical symmetry remains open. (2) Without $N(-\xi) = N(\xi)$ and momentum-independent $V_j$, unconditional $L^\infty$ normal-branch contraction at $A v_{\max} R \le 1/9$ for all $x > 0$ and the $C_\infty\sqrt{AM}$ (vs $\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{AM}$) asymmetric ceiling remain open.
