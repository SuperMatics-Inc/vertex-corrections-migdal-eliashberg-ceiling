## Model and method
The periodic one-dimensional dispersion is ξ(k) = 2|k|/π − 1 eV, −π ≤ k ≤ π. It is a lattice with long-range hopping (Fourier coefficients decay as 1/r²), not a nearest-neighbor cubic lattice. Its band is [−1,1] eV, the Fermi level is 0, εF=1 eV, and N(E)=1/2 eV⁻¹ per spin per site throughout the band. Thus the global normalized DOS maximum is exactly **A=1**, and M=λΩ². The Einstein interaction is V(iν)=2λΩ²/(Ω²+ν²), so N(0)V(0)=λ. The nonadiabatic parameters λΩ/εF range from 0.025 to 0.4; the largest is not asymptotically small. One-dimensional instabilities and corrections beyond the retained diagram are not assessed; these linearized truncated-equation results are not exact many-body transition temperatures.

The normal state retains the first crossed skeleton, and its linearized anomalous map retains all three propagator insertion positions (six scalar terms), adapting the full-bandwidth scheme of [Schrodi, Oppeneer and Aperis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12872) and the independently checked G1 implementation. Frequencies outside ±Nω are zero. `lattice.py` uses FFT momentum/frequency convolutions and a leading-eigenvalue root. N is the number of momentum points and Nω counts positive fermionic frequencies; N=4m+2 avoids placing a grid point exactly at the Fermi energy. Its independent nested momentum/frequency sum gives maximum absolute random-input discrepancy 1.11×10⁻¹⁶; comparisons of separately factored normal-u, normal-χ, and gap maps against the direct form give 3.47×10⁻¹⁸, 1.39×10⁻¹⁷ and 8.67×10⁻¹⁹, respectively (Mac audit exit 0). This is a discretized equation check, not an independent continuum validation.
