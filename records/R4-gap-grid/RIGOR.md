# First-crossed spherical stability: derivation and replay contract

## 1. Model and the static limit (PROVED)

Write $W=1$ after nondimensionalizing all energies, $x=\pi k_BT/W$, $w_n=|2n+1|$, $\eta=T/T_{\rm ceil}$ and $m=\Omega/W$. The per-spin DOS is $1/2$ for $-1<\xi<1$, so $K_j=\lambda/(1+4x^2j^2/m^2)$ and the dimensionless line weight is $g_j=(2x/\pi)K_j$. At fixed $x$ and $\lambda$, the ceiling ratio determines $m=x/(\pi C_\infty\eta\sqrt\lambda)$. Thus $K_j=\lambda/(1+\alpha j^2)$ where $\alpha=(2\pi C_\infty)^2\lambda\eta^2$. No evaluated decimal is substituted for the exact $C_\infty$; the interval in `replay.py` contains the certified interval in `../GAMMA/RESULT.md`.

Use $D=\{|p|\le1\}$, $d\mu=3d^3p/(4\pi)$, $\xi(p)=2|p|^3-1$, multiplication by $1_D$ on each electron line, and normalized full-space convolution $*$. We take the signed normal and anomalous equations of `../G1-SIGNED/RIGOR.md`, §1. The two transfer indices $s,t$ give electron indices $a=n-s$, $c=n-t$, $b=n-s-t$. Analogous momentum conservation gives $b=a+c-p$. As $m\to0$ at fixed $x$, $g_j\to g\delta_{j0}$, $g=2\lambda x/\pi$, with

$$\delta:=\sum_{j\ne0} g_j/g\;\le\;\pi^2m^2/(12x^2)=1/(12C_\infty^2\lambda\eta^2).$$

The limiting independent Matsubara equations on the inversion-even normal branch are

$$z_n=(ix(2n+1)-\xi-\Sigma_n)^{-1},\qquad \Sigma_n=g\int_D z_n\,d\mu+g^2(z_n*z_n*z_n)|_D.$$

Write $z_n=r_n-iQ_n$. The pairing operator in the symmetrized variable $d=|z|\phi$ is block diagonal in frequency. Its block kernel is

$$B_n(p,k)=|z_n(p)|\{g+g^2[3(r_n*r_n)(p-k)-(Q_n*Q_n)(p-k)]\}|z_n(k)|.$$

Indeed the two end insertions give $\Re(z*z)=r*r-Q*Q$ and the middle insertion gives $\Re(z*\bar z)=r*r+Q*Q$. The middle initially uses the sum of the external momenta; inversion of the integrated anomalous momentum reduces it to the difference on the inversion-even space. The normal derivative on the same space has kernel $[g+3g^2(z*z)(p-k)]z(k)^2$. None of these formulas restricts the perturbation to radial functions. A radial normal solution stays radial under the map, and uniqueness in a ball gives the continuation there.

The exact static identity $B_n(u_n|z_n|)=u_n|z_n|-x(2n+1)|z_n|$ follows by taking minus the imaginary part of the normal equation. A nonnegative block kernel would then give a Schur criterion with weight $|u_n z_n|$, but nonnegativity has not been proved for all $x$. On any common bounded line box, nonstatic remainder bounds for the maps and their first derivatives follow from the omitted one- and two-transfer weights $g\delta$ and $g^2(2\delta+\delta^2)$. Uniform convergence in that box, and local continuation wherever the normal Jacobian is invertible, concern fixed $x>0$; at $T=T_{\rm ceil}$, $m$ is instead proportional to $x$, so this is not a limit of the ceiling slice. No claim about omitted skeletons, phonon dressing or a physical transition follows.

## 2. Large-$x$ boxes, exact tails and a second DOS guard (PROVED)

Use $\|\Sigma\|_\infty\le hx$, $0<h<1$. Then $x|z_n|\le(w_n-h)^{-1}$. Put $A=2\lambda/(\pi x)$, $k_j=K_j/\lambda$, $Z=(1-h)^{-1}$, and $S_p=\sup_n\sum_j k_j(w_{n-j}-h)^{-p}$. The same formula for a convolution supremum applies to any nonincreasing profile in $w$. Products of the positive decreasing sequences are bounded by layer cake: the superlevel intervals of the transfer profile are centered at $n$, those of the fermion profile at $-1/2$. The count of their common integers is maximal at the nearest admissible centers $0,-1$. In that row retain positive-frequency indices $0\le m<N$ and their conjugates (or directly sum the $2N$ unfolded terms). For either summand of an omitted folded pair, $k_j\le1/(\alpha m^2)$ and $w_m-h\ge2m$. The combined tail is at most $2/[\alpha 2^p(p+1)(N-1)^{p+1}]$ by the integral test.

The supremum of the overlap mass $\int_{D^2}1_D(a+c-p)d\mu(a)d\mu(c)$ is $V=15/32$. The convolution of radial nonincreasing functions is radial nonincreasing by layer cake of balls: their overlap decreases with separation, by integrating coaxial slices perpendicular to the transverse plane (equivalently integrate the common lengths of two axial intervals at fixed transverse radius). Evaluate at $p=0$. The translated unit-ball overlap normalized by the single-ball volume is $1-3d/4+d^3/16$ for $0\le d\le1$, by the two caps of height $1-d/2$; integrating against $3d^2\,dd$ gives $15/32$.

The normal image in units of $x$ is bounded by $f=AS_1+VA^2 S_1^2Z$. Its derivative and the absolute row of the full pairing kernel are bounded by $b=AS_2+VA^2(2S_1S_2Z+S_1^2Z^2)$: bound the center line by $Z$, the ends by their own Matsubara profiles, and put the extra resolvent factor on the inserted line. Normal map contraction follows when $f<h$ and $b<1$, uniformly along scaling $K\mapsto \theta K$, $0\le\theta\le1$. The symmetric pairing Schur test uses $|z|$ as weight, making its weighted row just the unsymmetrized absolute row. These bounds hold on the full momentum and frequency spaces and show a sign-preserving, locally regular zero-coupling continuation. For the first guard, $A k_j$ and all products increase with $\lambda$ and decrease with $x$ and $\eta$. Therefore any successful first-box point also certifies the region above its $x,\eta$ and below its $\lambda$ (for finite $\eta$).

A second guard uses the exact bare momentum mean and spatial norms to tighten some rows. Let $d=xw$, $L=(w/(w-h))x\arctan(1/d)$ and $M=w x\arctan(1/d)/(w-h)^2$. The resolvent perturbation from the bare line gives $x|\int_D z\,d\mu|\le L$ (sum the bare mean and $hx\int |z_0|^2/(1-h/w)$, then multiply by $x$) and $x^2\int_D |z|^2\,d\mu\le M$, since $|z|\le (1-h/w)^{-1}|z_0|$. Let $S_L,S_M,S_{\sqrt M}$ denote the centered sums of these profiles. At a fixed center momentum $b$, Cauchy-Schwarz bounds the integral of the two end lines by their $L^2$ norms; integrate the center with $L^1\le L^2$. For an end insertion first fix its momentum and apply Cauchy-Schwarz to the other two factors; for a center insertion first fix $b$ and use Cauchy-Schwarz in $a$. The resulting bounds are

$$f=AS_L+A^2 S_{\sqrt M}^2\sqrt{M(1)},\qquad b=AS_M+A^2[2S_M S_{\sqrt M}\sqrt{M(1)}+S_{\sqrt M}^2 M(1)].$$

Use the same tails since $L,\sqrt M\le(w-h)^{-1}$ and $M\le(w-h)^{-2}$. One implementation uses exact rationals, $\arctan(1/u)\le\min(1/u,\pi/2,\pi/2-u+u^3/3)$ ($1/(1+u^2)\ge1-u^2$), an upper rational for $\pi/2$, and an integer square root. The other uses outward `mpmath.iv.atan2` and square roots, a different cutoff and unfolded sum. These guards use pointwise coupling monotonicity at fixed $x$, but no assertion of monotonicity for the true eigenvalue is needed.

## 3. Very small $x$ (PROVED with certified constants)

Use the absolute overlap and split normal contraction in `../G1-SIGNED/RIGOR.md` §4, not the under-review signed improvement. The exact transfer bound gives $R=\sum_j K_j\le2+1/(12 C_\infty^2)< B_0=23/5$ for $\eta\ge1$. The overlap bound is $L=2B_0^2 x J^3/(3\pi^2)$ with $J\le3+4\log(3/x)$. The majorant is increasing up to the endpoint $2^{-40}$, since $J>12$ there and its derivative after multiplication is $J^2(J-12)$. The normal split guards give $|b_n-b_n^*|\le\epsilon=2(1+8B_0)L$, where $b_n^*=(2/\pi)\arctan(1/u_n^*)$, and the sign-preserving widths obey $u_n/\omega_n<6$. Here $b_n=\int|Q_n|/(\pi N_0)$. All image and contraction conditions are checked at the endpoint, uniformly along the interaction homotopy.

For the G1-SIGNED mean block $I-S(b)$, keep the negative diagonal mass. Put $P_{nm}=K_{n-m}-K_{n+m+1}\ge0$ for $n,m\ge0$. The canceled diagonal of $S(1)$ is $(K_{2n+1}-2\sum_{j=1}^n K_j)/w_n$, using $\sum_m P_{nm}=K_0+2\sum_{j=1}^nK_j$. Use positive off-diagonals and replace this diagonal by $K_{2n+1}/w_n$. With weight $w_n^{-1/2}$, use the transfer envelope $2/(1+13j^2/5)$ (monotone in $\lambda$ before taking the endpoint $\lambda=2$ and $\eta=1$). For $n<16$, omit $m\ge64$ at cost $1/63^2$: $|m-n|\ge3m/4$, so the combined normalized entry is bounded by $(16/9+1)/(\gamma m^3)$, $\gamma=13/5$. For $n\ge16$, split at $m=n/2$. The $m\ge n/2$ part has $w_m\ge n$ and two-sided nonzero Toeplitz plus one-sided Hankel with $\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}\le2$, giving $12/(\gamma n)$; the $m<n/2$ part is at most $10/(\gamma n)$ using $|m-n|\ge n/2$, $n+m+1\ge n$, $w_m\ge1$ and at most $n$ terms. The exact and interval finite rows verify $\sup\sigma S(1)<0.95$.

Compare to $S(b)$ without deleting the mass: $b_n\le1+\epsilon$ adds at most $R\epsilon$ to the pairing Schur bound, including its $2K_{2n+1}$ positive diagonal after cancellation. The negative mass can add at most $R\epsilon+(2x/\pi)(R+R^2)$, since $1-b_m^*\le2x(w_m+R)/\pi$ and $\sum_m P_{nm}w_m/w_n=R$. To derive the latter identity, sum the unfolded signed linear row in $j=n-m$ symmetrically on $|j|\le N$, where the odd pieces cancel; fold the two fermion half-lines and pass to the limit (only finitely many endpoint terms for fixed $n$, each $O(1/N)$). The variance sector costs no loss. The crossed form costs $10L$ relative to $Q_0$ by G1-SIGNED §4; divide the residual form margin by 6 to bound the raw spectral gap relative to $\|d\|^2$. No uniformity outside this $x$ window is claimed.

## 4. Radial normal enclosures near the static limit

The centers in `static.py` are piecewise constant complex self-energies on dyadic radial cells; fixed-point iteration only proposes centers. For a radial function $f$ use $H_f(t)=tf(|t|)$ on the whole real line, zero outside its support. If $C=f*g$, spherical integration gives

$$r C(r)=\frac32\int_0^\infty H_f(s)\int_{|r-s|}^{r+s}H_g(t)\,dt\,ds,\qquad (rC)'=-\frac32(H_f*_{\mathbb R}H_g).$$

The derivative follows by differentiating the endpoints and extending $H$ oddly. For cellwise constant heights, the one-dimensional convolution is a linear tent sum with exact knot values equal to a discrete convolution times the mesh width. The first verifier uses the two complete odd arrays; the second assembles positive-positive convolution minus both shifted half-line correlations. Exact independent angular integration (split the inner primitive at all cell crossings) tests both at knots and in the interior. Integrate the linear interpolant exactly per cell to get $rC$; divide by $r$ (limit at the origin). If $l_i$ is $C$ at the left endpoint of cell $i$ and $d_i,d_{i+1}$ are derivative knots, then the range radius about $l_i$ is $\max(|d_i-l_i|,|d_{i+1}-l_i|)/(i+1)$. Indeed on a cell of fractional coordinate $s$ the residual in the numerator is bounded by $s h$ times that max, and $s/(i+s)\le1/(i+1)$. This justifies a continuum spatial bound rather than treating angular sampling as exact.

For cell midpoint $m_i$, use the resolvent identity and $|\xi'|\le6r_{\rm right}^2$ to majorize the step-height mismatch by $D_i|r-m_i|+E_i$ with $D_i=|z_i|+r_{\rm right}|z_i|\,z_{\max,i}\,6r_{\rm right}^2$ and $E_i=r_{\rm right}|z_i|\,z_{\max,i}\,\rho$, where $\rho$ is the cellwise uniform self-energy radius. A denominator lower bound over the cell and the ball verifies $z_{\max,i}$; all positive-frequency widths exceed $xw_n/2$. Add arithmetic rounding envelopes to these bounds. The $L^2(d\mu)$ error of $z$ relative to the function represented by the step heights is at most $\sqrt{3h}(h\|D\|_2/\sqrt{12}+\|E\|_2)$, and the $L^1$ error is bounded with $3h\sum_i r_{{\rm right},i}(hD_i/4+E_i)$. The discrete norms of the heights compute the exact $L^2(d\mu)$ norm of their represented functions before arithmetic rounding. These formulas do not assert a bound on pointwise values of the step-height representation at $r=0$.

For a double convolution, telescope with Cauchy-Schwarz to get an $L^\infty$ error at most $e(2a+e)$, $a$ the step-line $L^2$ norm and $e$ its $L^2$ error (also for the real and imaginary parts with their respective norms). For a triple convolution telescope with the substituted error in $L^1$ and the other two factors in $L^2$, giving $3e_1(a+e)^2$. When re-stepping the approximate pair's height use its derivative envelope, midpoint approximation and support of length 2; the $L^2$ bound is obtained by integrating the squared linear deviation ($h^2/12$) and adding the padded height error in norm. Use Cauchy-Schwarz with the final line. The integral of one line is bounded using $L^1$ and the midpoint-weighted sum for the step heights. Pairing and normal derivative rows are bounded by taking the entrywise maximum across the coupling endpoints for $|1+gC|$, adding $g$ times the error in $C$, and convolving the resulting positive step upper kernel with the step upper $|z|^2$. Their radial output envelopes apply to every external momentum, without projection of the pairing eigenfunction.

For each rescaled positive block use $x'=xw$, $\lambda'=\lambda/w$ (so $g$ is unchanged), and the complex conjugate at negative frequency. A closed ball of radius $\rho$ with center $c$ is admitted on a whole coupling slab if $q<1$ and the residual at $c$ is less than $(1-q)\rho$, where $q$ bounds the Fréchet derivative on the ball. The next ball contains the preceding endpoint fixed point by using the residual divided by $1-q$ and the inter-center distance; hence successive local solutions continue the same branch. Global product boxes use these radii separately; any remaining static blocks use the bare peak guard, decreasing in $w$, at all couplings. The norms for this argument are absolute self-energy norms, not norms normalized by $w_n$.

In the same product box, $\|z\|_\infty\le2/x$, including the tail. The missing nonstatic transfers have normal residual in units of $x$ at most $2A\delta+8VA^2(2\delta+\delta^2)$. Their normal derivative and absolute pairing row are bounded by $4A\delta+48VA^2(2\delta+\delta^2)$. The guards add these to each static residual and derivative, retaining the full Matsubara line for every $\eta\ge2^{18}$ (including $\eta=\infty$). The same rectangles and overlaps then bound the coupled continuation and its pairing spectrum, not merely a perturbation to a truncated eigenvalue.

**Arithmetic trust base.** IEEE binary64 with round-to-nearest and accurately rounded elementary operations; `iv` scalar coupling endpoints are widened by `nextafter`. FFT/iteration output is never used without a fresh residual and row check. Cells are dyadic, at most 4096. The direct/reference convolutions operate on linear arrays; a complex product/accumulation uses at most 8 rounded scalar operations and each output uses at most 8192 such products. Two half-line correlations and one convolution involve no greater total product count than the full two-sided product; subtraction adds two operations. With $u=2^{-53}$, $\gamma_{2^{17}}<2^{-35}$ where $\gamma_k=ku/(1-ku)$. The convolution has per-output absolute error bounded by this factor times the sum of absolute products. In the derivative knots, multiplication by $3h/2$ makes the sum at most $6\max|a_i|\max|b_i|$ (supports of the positive arrays are at most 2). In the primitive, summing the trapezoids and dividing by $r$ averages the knot errors; the factor from the running sum remains bounded by the same $\gamma_{2^{17}}$. Thus $2^{-26}(1+\max|a_i|)(1+\max|b_i|)$ exceeds the absolute errors in the quotient, derivative and range primitives, with a further length factor for height errors. Similarly positive dot products have relative error at most $\gamma_{2^{17}}$; the mean uses an absolute error scale bounded by the height norm by Cauchy-Schwarz. The bare real dispersion error is at most $8u$ before subtracting the proposed center, and the admitted imaginary denominator is at least $1/32$; complex division errors and absolute-value errors are covered by the explicit pads in `moments`. The remaining nonnegative error-envelope additions, norm calculations and branch guard products add far fewer than $2^{17}$ rounded operations on any path and are rounded out with `up` or a reduced right-hand side. This verifier is not a formal proof of NumPy or a Lean proof.

For the signed path, all expressions after the convolution contain fewer than 64 elementary operations per entry, excluding maxima (exact selection of represented entries). For the computed binary64 midpoint $g$, use $\operatorname{nextafter}(\max(g-g_{\rm lo},g_{\rm hi}-g),+\infty)$, not half the real interval length. Thus the interval-width term already covers midpoint rounding, and is applied to the same represented $g$ used in the residual. At each residual entry the `SIGN_PAD` $2^{-42}$ multiplies $1+g_{\rm hi}|m_z|+g_{\rm hi}^2\max(|{\tt outside}|+{\tt var})+\|\sigma\|_\infty$, not the small canceled residual. In the signed row, the arithmetic error of $3rr-qq$ is at most $\gamma_{8}(3|rr|+|qq|)$; the normal row uses $3|zz|$ instead. The evaluation of $1+g\,{\tt corr}$ and its complex modulus at each endpoint is paid together with that error by $2^{-42}(1+g_{\rm hi}{\tt pre\_cancel})$. The two endpoint norms bound the entire coupling interval by convexity. Independent convolution and line-model errors remain in the nonnegative `dev` and `extra` terms, and the subsequent nonnegative arithmetic is outward-padded by `up`. This assumes no flush-to-zero or unsafe reassociation and elementary complex magnitudes accurate within two binary64 ulps; underflows in relevant nonnegative scales are below the absolute pad.

## 5. Static positivity criterion and separate numerical diagnostic

There is a simple sufficient static positivity condition that avoids assuming a positive vertex. For any real radial $r_n$, the Fourier representation of its autocorrelation is the spherical average of $|\widehat r_n|^2$. The averaging factor is $\sin s/s\ge-1/\pi$, since the sine is nonnegative up to $\pi$ and has magnitude at most one afterwards. Thus $(r_n*r_n)(p-k)\ge-\|r_n\|_2^2/\pi$ in the normalized measure. Cauchy-Schwarz gives $(Q_n*Q_n)(p-k)\le\|Q_n\|_2^2$. Consequently the unsymmetrized static vertex is pointwise nonnegative if $g\|z_n\|_2^2\le1$. If this is known on every block, with uniform positive-width bounds and a uniform upper bound on $u_n/\omega_n$, the static identity in §1 and Schur give strict stability. This is a conditional criterion, not an assertion that the normalized branch meets it on the entire interval.

`spectral.py` is independent of the spatial step enclosures and is **NUMERICAL**. It uses $\widehat f(q)=3\int r^2 f(r)j_0(qr)dr$, so the inverse weights on a spectral grid of spacing $\pi/L$ are $2q^2/(3L)$. The sine-transform scale is equivalent to this quadrature. Momentum products use convolution implemented by multiplication of transforms; the crossed kernel retains both end insertions and the middle insertion with the conjugated line. The singlet projection folds positive and negative frequencies, and symmetrizes by $|z|$ and the square root of radial quadrature weights. For higher even angular momentum the addition formula for $j_0(q|p-k|)$ replaces the two inverse factors by $j_\ell(qp)j_\ell(qk)$; the rainbow term is only in $\ell=0$. This implementation checks matrix symmetry and positive normal widths at each reported solve. Its finite quadrature, finite transform box, discarded frequency tail and finite-step normal continuation have no error certification; refinement is a diagnostic only.

## 6. Finite Einstein grid: signed normal enclosure (PROVED criterion, CERTIFIED nodes)

Scale $S=\Sigma/x$, $Z=xz$, $\ell_j=(2\lambda/(\pi x))/(1+\alpha j^2)$, and multiply $\ell$ by $\theta\in[0,1]$. The scaled normal map $F_\theta$ consists of the rainbow $\theta\sum_j\ell_j\int Z_{n-j}$ and the crossed term $\theta^2\sum_{s,t}\ell_s\ell_t(Z_{n-s}*Z_{n-s-t}*Z_{n-t})|_D$. We verify complex sup-norm tubes about momentum-constant, purely imaginary centers $S_{c,n}=-i d_n$ for $n\ge0$, reflected by conjugation; the tube allows momentum-dependent real and imaginary parts. The trial $d_n\ge0$ vanishes for $n\ge N=96$, is zero at zero interaction, and is affine between consecutive homotopy anchors. Only the verifier, not the iteration that proposes $d$, is used to bound the defect. The norm divides each row by $\omega_n=1$ if $|2n+1|<17$ and by 4 otherwise.

**Signed center defect.** For $v_n=2n+1+d_n$ on the positive line, the flat-DOS mean at the center is $-iM(v_n)$, $M(v)=x\arctan(1/(xv))$. Its rainbow row uses the **difference** $P_{nm}=\ell_{n-m}-\ell_{n+m+1}\ge0$. Enclose $\theta \sum_m P_{nm} M(v_m)-d_n$ separately at both endpoints; add a curvature allowance $\sup|f''|/8$ in the unit slab coordinate to the endpoint hull. This interpolation error follows by comparison with the two quadratic functions with second derivative $\pm\sup|f''|$ and zero endpoint values. The product-rule terms in the allowance are $2\Delta\theta |M'|\,|\Delta d_m|+\theta_{\rm hi}|M''|\,|\Delta d_m|^2$; $|M'|=(v^2+x^{-2})^{-1}$ and $|M''|=2v/(v^2+x^{-2})^2$. The tail of the signed sum can contribute at most its positive width, while for output $n\ge N$ the center vanishes and the absolute rainbow bound suffices. Add the absolute **complex** crossed image to the defect; no cancellation of its real or imaginary parts is assumed.

**Line and crossed estimates.** At the smallest center width $v$ in a slab, put $u=v-\rho\omega$, $\beta=u/v$. With $y=\xi/x$,

$$|Z|^2\le \big(\sqrt{v^2+y^2}-\rho\omega\big)^{-2}\le(u^2+\beta y^2)^{-1}.$$

The second inequality follows by $\sqrt{v^2+y^2}\le v+y^2/(2v)$. Integrate the Lorentz majorant for the $L^1,L^2,L^\infty$ norms of $Z$ and for the $L^1,L^2,L^\infty$ norms of $Z^2$ against the exact uniform DOS. For example, $\int |Z|^2 \le x\arctan(\sqrt\beta/(xu))/(u\sqrt\beta)$, clipped by $u^{-2}$. At fixed momentum of any one line in the triple convolution, the product of the other two integrates to at most the product of their $L^2$ norms, by translation invariance and Cauchy-Schwarz on zero-extended lines. Thus each treatment of the fixed line gives an $L^1,L^2,L^2$ product, and the fourth guard is $V$ times the three peaks. On derivative rows apply these bounds separately to each of the three insertions with $Z^2$ in place of $Z$ on the inserted line, weighted by $\omega$ for the normal norm. Pairing uses weight $|Z|$ in Schur on the symmetric full kernel, giving the unweighted version of the same row estimate; conjugation and taking real parts do not increase the bounds. This controls the operator on all allowed angular sectors, not merely a discretization or the radial sector.

**Infinite Matsubara sums.** The primary resolves transfers $|s|\le J=192$ on $n<N$ and bounds the omitted transfer mass by $2\ell_0/(\alpha J)$, with $|2(n-s)+1|\ge2(J+1)-(2N-1)$ there. For $n\ge N$, separate $|2m+1|<N$ (then $|n-m|\ge N/2$) from the complementary profiles bounded by their inverse bare-width envelopes. The independent implementation folds to nonnegative $m<M=297$; the omitted transfer mass is bounded by $2\ell_0/(\alpha(M-N))$, and the omitted electron widths have $|2m+1|\ge2M+1$. Its large-row split uses $|2m+1|<5N/4$, with the same distance argument. In both, global profile maxima cover the whole zero-center tail since the bulks include its first row; the explicit tail terms apply the bare width minus $4\rho$ and the same inverse power as the profile. For crossed terms the first eight rows resolve a square of transfer pairs directly ($|s|,|t|\le7$ or 9), using $n-s,n-s-t,n-t$ separately. On the remaining pairs factor the one-line transfer sums (bounding the middle line by a global profile maximum) and split the union of the two wings without double-counting. In the other rows the factored guard covers the whole transfer plane. The two end-insertion row bounds agree by $s\leftrightarrow t$.

**Regularity.** In a closed tube of radius $\rho$ let $r$ bound the defect and $q$ the derivative. The direct guard is $r<(1-q)\rho$, $q<1$. For some slabs use instead the derivative $L=D F_{\rm rainbow}(S_c)$ at the center. Its range is momentum-constant and on that subspace the DOS moment is $\int Z_c^2=-1/(v^2+x^{-2})$, not $\int|Z_c|^2$. If $l$ bounds $\|L\|$ and $k$ bounds its norm on constants, then $\|L^2\|\le lk$ and $\|(I-L)^{-1}\|\le(1+l)/(1-lk)$ if $lk<1$. Apply the direct test to $G(S)=S+(I-L)^{-1}(F_\theta(S)-S)$. The bound on $D F_{\rm rainbow}(S)-L$ follows from $|Z^2-Z_c^2|\le\rho\omega |Z||Z_c|(|Z|+|Z_c|)$ and spatial Cauchy-Schwarz; the crossed derivative is bounded as above. Both types of tube have strictly positive widths. At a common slab endpoint the two balls share the same center and are nested, so their fixed points agree by uniqueness in the larger ball. Since $I-D F_\theta=(I-L)(I-D G)$ in the preconditioned case, both tests certify local regularity along the zero-coupling branch. At $\theta=1$ the proof also bounds the pairing spectral top.

**Arithmetic and replay.** Both paths widen `iv` scalar endpoints for $\ell_0$ and $\alpha$. They operate on dyadic $x$, the represented centers/radii, and outward neighbors of the rational slab endpoints. The signed residual is rounded at the positive rainbow scale *before* subtracting the center; the independent reflected coefficient subtracts separately outward transfer endpoints whereas the primary measures the difference by a nonnegative numerator. The wings in the independent path subtract a lower panel mass from an upper all-transfer mass. The primary uses unfolded transfer cuts and binary64 nonnegative sums; the independent one folds the two half-lines and computes the main sums by exact checked `int64` dots after outward quantization in multiples of $2^{-22}$. Its `atan` uses two half-angle reductions and a polynomial through degree 27: the reduced argument is at most $\tan(\pi/16)<1/5$, so the error before floating rounding is at most $4(1/5)^{29}/29<2^{-64}$.
Before the independent integer dot for the inverse bound, the center width is enclosed downward and $\omega/(v^2+x^{-2})$ upward. The integer ceiling is applied only after that enclosure; a nearest-rounded quotient can itself lie on the wrong side of an integer boundary.

The remaining arithmetic uses round-to-nearest binary64 and accurately rounded elementary functions; no BLAS accuracy beyond a conventional binary64 product/sum rounding bound is assumed. Nonnegative binary64 reductions use at most 385 entries, and the roundoff path (per-term factors plus reduction) between pads has length below 2048. Thus $\gamma_{2048}<2^{-41}$ for the summations. On the grid $\ell_0<21$, $\ell_0/\alpha<8$ and each center update is bounded by $\ell_0+4\ell_0/\alpha<53$, up to its rounding pad. So head $v<650$, $u>1/8$, $\sqrt{\beta}>1/73$, and $1/16\le x\le16$. In the independent $L^1$ primitive, the rounded argument of $\log(y+\sqrt{1+y^2})$ has relative error below $16\cdot2^{-53}$; the log has absolute error of that order even at small $y$. The multiplier $x/\sqrt{\beta}<1168$ keeps its contribution below $2^{-38}$ before padding. ## 7. Finite Einstein grid: momentum-dependent normal enclosures

**PROVED criterion.** Use \(S,Z,\ell,\theta\) from §6, and write \(w_n=|2n+1|\). The normal Banach space consists of bounded \(S/w\) on the band, with inversion and complex conjugate frequency reflection enforced. Let \(c\) be a radial dyadic cellwise constant center on \(0\le n<N\), zero for \(n\ge N\). The enclosure ball itself permits arbitrary inversion-even, not only radial, functions. The renormalized positive-line width at each center is at least \(b w_n\) for a verified \(b>1/4\); in a displacement of norm \(a<b\), the resolvent exists and its relative change is bounded by \((a/b)/(1-a/b)\). The bare solution is the leftmost center. The floating sine-transform solve proposes the anchors, but is not used to evaluate any guard.

For a fixed left endpoint \((c,\theta_0)\), use \(V h=(\int_D Z_c^2 h_n/w_n)_{0\le n<N}\) and let \(J\) be the head-to-head rainbow transfer, with column-row weights \(w_m/w_n\) and with the reflected line complex conjugated. We identify a normalized head vector with the corresponding signed-frequency, spatially constant output (supported only on the head). The finite part of the scaled rainbow derivative is \(JV\); \(D=VJ\) in the defect computation is obtained by multiplying \(J\) by the diagonal complex moments \(\int Z_c^2\). Explicitly, writing \(X=\operatorname{diag}\int Z_c^2\), the correction \(A=I+MV\) uses a dyadic approximation \(M\) to \(J(I-XJ)^{-1}\), and

$$I-A(I-JV)=(J-M+MXJ)V.$$

Only the right-hand identity is needed to verify the approximate inverse. The rounded products are enclosed entrywise or by complex \(2\times2\) Frobenius blocks; each input uses \(|Vh|_n\le\int |Z_c|^2\|h_n/w_n\|_\infty\). The remaining rainbow derivative at the left endpoint is bounded explicitly on omitted head columns and by the full bound on the tail rows. If \(\|I-A(I-JV)\|<1\), then \(A\) is surjective; as the identity plus a finite-rank operator it is also injective. The primary uses a transposed linear solve and dyadic entries with denominator \(2^{36}\), the second uses separately assembled real and imaginary matrices and denominator \(2^{34}\). Neither uses the floating-point inverse as an exact inverse.
The newer block check takes the smaller of the Frobenius norm and \(\sqrt{\|B\|_1\|B\|_\infty}\) of the entrywise absolute \(2\times2\) block, with outward bounds for both. It can shrink but cannot enlarge the original block bound. Radius proposals are checked directly by the strict guard; the denser proposal set appends to, and does not replace, the original candidates.

On a slab, interpolate \(c\) between two exact represented dyadic centers and \(\theta\) between its two represented endpoints (the successive slabs cover \([0,1]\)). Write \(t=\Delta\theta\ge0\), \(d=\|\Delta c/w\|_\infty/b<0.2\). Let \(R_0,C_0\) bound the bare rainbow and cubic at the left center before the respective \(\theta,\theta^2\) factors. In the unit slab coordinate, the residual at the affine center is bounded by the larger of the endpoint defects plus

$$\frac18\left[\frac{2td+2\theta_1d^2}{(1-d)^3}R_0+
\frac{2t^2+12\theta_1td+12\theta_1^2d^2}{(1-d)^5}C_0\right].$$

Here the second derivative of a product of three resolvents contains three second derivatives and six ordered products of two first derivatives, totaling the coefficient 12. The displayed bounds are applied rowwise in the normalized norm, then multiplied by the bound for \(|A|\). With ball radius \(\rho\), let \(e=(\|\Delta c/w\|_\infty+\rho)/b<0.30\) and \(k=(1-e)^{-2}-1\). Relative to the left center, the change of the product of two resolvents, including conjugations, is bounded by \(k\) times their original absolute product. A squared inserted line obeys the same bound. For the crossed derivative, keep the signed two-line coefficient when charging the change of the inserted squared line; the variation of the two coefficient lines is bounded term by term and multiplied by the perturbed squared line. Thus the extra normal derivative is bounded by

$$k\left[\theta_1 R_1+\theta_1^2\bigl(C_{1,s}+(1-e)^{-2}C_1\bigr)\right],$$

where \(R_1,C_1\) are absolute derivative rows and \(C_{1,s}\) retains signed grouping. Add the preconditioned left-center signed derivative, the omitted rainbow part, \(tR_1\), and the inverse defect to obtain the full guard \(q\) for \(S\mapsto S+A(F_\theta(S)-S)\). With preconditioned residual \(\epsilon\), \(\epsilon+\rho q<\rho\), \(q<1\) proves a unique fixed point in the ball, and its true normal Jacobian is invertible by Neumann's series. At a common endpoint the two balls have exactly the same center, so uniqueness in the larger ball identifies the roots. The distance at the right endpoint is at most the preconditioned right defect divided by \(1-q\). The widths stay positive, and rotational symmetry plus local uniqueness gives radial normal solutions.

**Spatial guards.** The two correlation paths in §4 are used on different meshes, with the second splitting each center cell into two. Resolvent error, midpoint curvature, and the cellwise positive peak bound for \(Z\) enclose the continuum spatial integrals. The first recomputes the complex DOS moments of \(Z,Z^2\) by composite midpoint with the shell curvature. The second integrates \(Z^2\) per shell as the shell mass divided by the product of the two complex endpoint denominators. With shell midpoint denominator \(d_0\) and half-width \(h_\xi\) in the dispersion \(\xi/x\), the moment of \(Z\) is shell mass times \(d_0^{-1}\sum_{k\ge0}(h_\xi/d_0)^{2k}/(2k+1)\); the geometric remainder beyond 15 terms is bounded explicitly. The second uses exact `int64` products for the folded correlations after separate dyadic quantization of the real and imaginary step heights. It checks integer accumulation capacity and charges the input quantization error before doing signed operations. Signed kernels are grouped by the frequency of the inserted line before absolute values; the two crossed end coefficients have \(Z_a*Z_b\), and the middle has \(Z_a*Z_c\) in the normal derivative and \(\Re(Z_a*\overline Z_c)\) in the pairing vertex. **Frequency tails and pairing.** Only the low output rows evaluate the signed crossed coefficient. Other outputs use the absolute whole cubic and its whole derivative. **Optional tighter moment guards.** `radial_moments.py` retains §7's check but can replace the two-line error by a cell-moment bound. For the inner-vertex change, let \(r_m\) be an upper bound for \(b w_m\sup_p|Z_m(p)|\), clipped at one. The relative excursion in line \(m\) is at most \(e r_m\). For two line factors, \(f(u,v)=1/[(1-u)(1-v)]-1\) is separately convex.
